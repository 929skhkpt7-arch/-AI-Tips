Bottom Line

1. Is the income durable?

Yes, with an important qualification. The distribution mechanism is durable because it is tied to NAV and supported by a portfolio that has historically generated returns well above 8%. But the payout is not equivalent to a corporation's recurring free-cash-flow dividend; it is partly funded by realized capital gains and can potentially include return of capital.

2. Is today's valuation attractive?

Not especially. The fund is essentially trading at NAV, versus a roughly 13% 10-year average discount and approximately 12% five-year average discount according to available historical data.

3. Does ADX deserve the next investment dollar over comparable alternatives?

At $25–26, not quite. At $22–24.50, yes. ADX is probably the highest-quality durable-income CEF of the group, but its current price has already captured much of the improvement investors once received from buying it at a discount. The best opportunity is therefore to own ADX, but buy it opportunistically rather than chase the ~8% headline distribution.
